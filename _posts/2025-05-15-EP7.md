<p>An invasive plant on the shores of Utah’s Great Salt Lake is causing an unexpected problem – it’s draining freshwater from a system already under drought stress and potentially turning the lake even saltier. Hear from two scientists with expertise in hydrogeology working together to find out exactly how much latent heat, or evapotranspiration, is in flux over this incredible wetland habitat.</p>

He has built 11 flux stations in Utah with his colleague <a hef= "https://www.linkedin.com/in/kathryn-ladig-34585a13a/">Kathryn Ladig</a>. Together, they have worked collaboratively with universities; federal government (US Bureau of Reclamation); flux networks like ameriflux, fluxnet, mesowest, and phenocam; and more. He also maintains the AmeriFlux site, Great Salt Lake Phragmites, abbreviated US-UTP, which is part of the Utah Flux Network (<a href = "https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/the-utah-flux-network/">UFLUX</a>)</figcaption>
