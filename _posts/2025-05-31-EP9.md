<p>Wildfires and clear-cutting in the boreal and arctic regions can release harmful gasses into the atmosphere. Experts in fluxes from taiga and tundra ecosystems share their first-hand fieldwork experiences in this episode about the atmospheric consequences of increasingly frequent and intensifying fires.</p>

      <figcaption><b><a href = "https://www.linkedin.com/in/mark-isbell-a0897560/">Anastasia Makhnykina</a></b> is a researcher in <a href = "https://cseees.unc.edu/event/climate-research-siberia/">experimental & applied ecology</a> at the Sukachev Institute of Forest in Krasnoyarsk, Russia studying CO2 fluxes from wildfires in boreal forests. More than one-fifth of the world’s forests are found in Russia, which has a larger forested region than the Amazon, composed of mostly birch, pine, spruce, and fir trees. Its forests make up <a href = "https://wwfeu.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/russia_forest_cc_final_13nov07.pdf">11% of global biomass</a>, making CO2 and methane storage in these ecosystems crucial for regulating the global climate.</figcaption>
